# Example Life Insurance Program — Compliance Risk Watchlist

**Program:** Example Life Insurance Program (Example Life/Partner Org)  
**Last Updated:** 2025  
**Purpose:** Identifies the top compliance risks specific to this program's marketing materials and distribution channels. Use this watchlist in every creative review and prior to campaign launch.

---

## Risk Severity Key

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CRITICAL | Regulatory violation or enforcement exposure; material must not run without correction |
| HIGH | Significant non-compliance risk; requires mandatory correction before approval |
| MEDIUM | Potential violation; requires legal/compliance judgment call before approval |
| LOW | Best practice gap; recommend correction but may be approved with documented rationale |

---

## RISK 1 — Graded Benefit Misrepresentation (Guaranteed Issue)

**Severity:** CRITICAL  
**Product:** Guaranteed Issue Final Expense  
**Standards:** NAIC Model Advertising Reg IND-06; FTC FED-01; WA ST-WA-01

### The Risk
Guaranteed Issue products lead with "guaranteed" and "no health questions required" — powerful claims for an audience with health concerns. The mandatory 3-year waiting period (graded benefit) means beneficiaries receive only 120% of premiums paid — not the full death benefit — if the insured dies of natural causes within the first 3 policy years. If this limitation is buried, minimized, or absent, the advertising creates a materially false impression.

### What Triggers This Risk
- Headline or primary call-to-action uses "guaranteed issue" or "no health questions" without adjacent graded benefit disclosure
- Waiting period disclosure appears only in fine print, asterisk, or below the fold
- Visual design draws eye to "guaranteed" and away from limitations
- Digital ads (banner, social) that lack sufficient space for disclosure and omit waiting period entirely
- Testimonials or scenarios that imply full benefit was received without mentioning the waiting period

### Required Control
> **Mandatory disclosure in all Guaranteed Issue materials:**  
> *"This policy has a waiting period. If the insured dies of natural causes in the first [3] years, the benefit paid to your beneficiaries will be [120%] of the premiums you have paid, not the full death benefit."*  
> (Adapt to current policy form language; placement must be prominent — not footnoted below visual)

## RISK 2 — Term Life Premium Escalation Not Disclosed

**Severity:** CRITICAL  
**Product:** Step-Rate Term Life  
**Standards:** NAIC IND-05; FTC FED-01, FED-02; NY ST-NY-02

### The Risk
Term life premiums increase every time the insured enters a new 5-year age band. A consumer who enters at age 55 paying $63/month will pay $97 at 60–64, $152 at 65–69, and so on. If advertising promotes a low initial premium without disclosing this escalation structure, it creates a materially false impression of cost.

### What Triggers This Risk
- Rate tables or "starting at" rates shown without note that rates increase with age
- Direct mail or landing pages showing only the applicant's current age rate without adjacent disclosure of future increases
- Headline claims like "low monthly rates" without rate escalation disclosure
- Long-form copy that mentions premium structure in a way that could be misread as level

### Required Control
> **Mandatory disclosure in all Term Life rate advertising:**  
> *"Rates increase when you enter each new 5-year age band."*  
> Or equivalent — must appear in proximity to any quoted or implied rate.

## RISK 3 — Coverage Amount Ceiling Not Communicated

**Severity:** HIGH  
**Products:** All products  
**Standards:** NAIC IND-01; FTC FED-01, FED-02

### The Risk
Standard online limits are $125,000 (Term), $75,000 (Whole Life), and $25,000 (Guaranteed Issue). If materials emphasize "get the coverage you need" or imply unlimited or high coverage without stating actual limits, they create a false impression about coverage capacity.

### What Triggers This Risk
- Phrases like "coverage for whatever you leave behind" without ceiling statement
- Visuals of large estates, expensive final expenses, etc. without context on coverage limits
- Benefit calculators or needs estimators that produce outputs higher than available coverage without a caveat

### Required Control
State actual coverage maximum or include: *"Coverage amounts up to $[X] available online. Higher amounts may be available by calling [number]."*

## RISK 4 — "Guaranteed" Language Without Limitation Disclosure

**Severity:** CRITICAL  
**Products:** Guaranteed Issue Final Expense  
**Standards:** NAIC IND-01, IND-06; FTC FED-01, FED-02

### The Risk
The word "guaranteed" appears in the product name and is a central marketing message. Regulatory guidance consistently treats stand-alone "guaranteed" claims in insurance advertising as inherently misleading unless limitations are disclosed with equal prominence. This risk extends beyond just the waiting period — it includes guaranteed issue claims without disclosing that "natural causes" deaths in years 1–3 do not receive the full benefit.

### What Triggers This Risk
- "Guaranteed issue" in headline without graded benefit in same visual field
- "Your acceptance is guaranteed" in body copy without adjacent limitation
- Social media posts or display ads that use "guaranteed" as the only copy and link to a landing page with buried disclosures

### Required Control
Treat "guaranteed" as a trigger word requiring adjacent limitation disclosure in all placements, including space-constrained digital formats.

## RISK 5 — Partner Org Endorsement / "Best for Seniors" Claims

**Severity:** HIGH  
**Products:** All products  
**Standards:** FTC FED-03; CO-05; FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255)

### The Risk
Consumers often read an association's name on an insurance offer as an independent seal of approval. If materials present Partner Org-branded coverage as "best for older adults" or "recommended" while omitting that Partner Org receives a licensing fee from Example Life, a regulator or private plaintiff could argue the endorsement is deceptive. The exposure grows with superlatives and with any suggestion that Partner Org compared insurers on members' behalf.

The FTC's endorsement guidelines require disclosure of material connections between an endorser and advertiser. Partner Org's licensing-fee interest is a material connection.

### What Triggers This Risk
- "Partner Org-endorsed," "Partner Org-recommended," "Partner Org's preferred" without disclosure of licensing relationship
- Implication that Partner Org independently evaluated and selected Example Life as the best insurer for seniors
- Partner Org branding used in a way that implies Partner Org is acting as an objective consumer advocate rather than a licensing partner

### Required Control
All materials should accurately characterize the relationship. Recommended disclosure context:  
*"The Example Life Insurance Program is issued by Example Life Insurance Company. Partner Org receives a licensing fee from Example Life for use of the Partner Org name and logo. These fees help fund Partner Org's member programs."*  
(Adapt to current disclosure language approved by Partner Org legal)

## RISK 6 — Fear, Urgency, and Decline Framing

**Severity:** HIGH  
**Products:** All products  
**Channels:** Direct mail, call center, digital  
**Standards:** FTC FED-01; Partner Org brand standards; CA ST-CA-01; FL ST-FL-01; state AG senior marketing guidance

### The Risk
Partner Org brand standards prohibit marketing that portrays older adults as confused, incapacitated, frightened, or in decline. Marketing that uses fear of death, health decline, or financial ruin as primary motivators — without providing balanced, empowering framing — violates both Partner Org brand standards and FTC senior marketing guidance. Some states (CA, FL) have elder financial protection statutes that treat fear-based marketing as a form of financial exploitation.

### What Triggers This Risk
- Imagery of frail elders, hospital settings, grief, or family financial ruin as primary visual
- Headlines like "Don't leave your family with nothing" or "What happens to your family when you're gone?"
- Countdown clocks or limited-time enrollment urgency in direct mail or digital ads without a genuine enrollment window
- "Your age could disqualify you soon" or similar deadline pressure
- Call center scripts that reference health vulnerability as a primary reason to buy now

### Required Control
- Imagery must show older adults as active, capable, and engaged (per Partner Org brand standards)
- Urgency claims must be based on genuine, verifiable enrollment windows — not manufactured scarcity
- Reframe: benefits-forward, empowerment-oriented language
- Escalate any script or concept using health fear or family distress as primary driver to legal review

## RISK 7 — Partner Org Membership Requirement Not Disclosed

**Severity:** HIGH  
**Products:** All products  
**Standards:** NAIC IND-01; FTC FED-01, FED-02

### The Risk
Partner Org membership is required to purchase coverage. If advertising presents coverage as broadly available to adults 55+ without disclosing the membership requirement, it creates a false impression and generates applicant confusion, downstream customer service issues, and potential regulatory complaints.

### What Triggers This Risk
- "Available to adults age 55 and older" without mentioning Partner Org membership requirement
- Landing page or quote flow that collects personal information before surfacing membership requirement
- Teaser advertising that drives to example-co.com without mentioning Partner Org membership

### Required Control
State Partner Org membership requirement in all advertising, not only at application stage.  
*"Available to Partner Org members age 55 and older."*

## RISK 8 — Accelerated Benefit Rider Misframing (Accelerated Death Benefit)

**Severity:** HIGH  
**Product:** Whole Life with Accelerated Benefit Rider  
**Standards:** NAIC IND-01; FTC FED-01, FED-02; CO-07

### The Risk
The accelerated benefits feature allows access to up to 40% of the face amount while alive — but only upon diagnosis of a qualifying illness. It is not a savings account, cash value access, or general-purpose benefit. Framing it as broadly accessible cash or a financial planning tool without disclosing the qualifying illness requirement is misleading.

Additionally, this product is not available in all states. Using it in a national campaign without state availability disclosure risks advertising a product that is not available to a portion of the audience.

### What Triggers This Risk
- "Access your benefit while you're still alive" without "upon qualifying illness" modifier
- Framing as "accelerated benefit" (brand name) without explaining the triggering condition
- National campaign materials for this product without state availability disclaimer
- Comparison to cash value life insurance without disclosing the qualifying illness restriction

### Required Control
Always pair "accelerated benefit" with "if you are diagnosed with a qualifying illness" in the same sentence or adjacent disclosure.  
State availability disclaimer required in any national placement.

## RISK 9 — State Availability Omissions

**Severity:** CRITICAL for MT; HIGH for WA age bands  
**Products:** Guaranteed Issue (MT, WA); Accelerated Benefit Rider (multi-state)  
**Standards:** ST-MT-01, ST-MT-02; ST-WA-01; CO-07

### The Risk
Guaranteed Issue Final Expense is not available in Montana. Guaranteed Issue has different age eligibility in Washington (55–75 vs. 55–85 in most states). Materials that fail to reflect these restrictions expose Example Life and Partner Org to regulatory complaints and consumer harm.

### What Triggers This Risk
- National direct mail piece for Guaranteed Issue mailed to MT residents
- Online landing page for Guaranteed Issue without MT exclusion
- Age band stated as "ages 55 to 85" without noting WA's 55–75 restriction
- Quote tool that allows MT or age-75+ WA consumers to advance through the application for Guaranteed Issue

### Required Control
- All Guaranteed Issue materials must include: *"Not available in Montana."*
- WA-targeted materials must use WA-specific age bands (55–75)
- Digital application must block ineligible applicants at intake, not at submission

## RISK 10 — Replacement / Inducement to Lapse Existing Coverage

**Severity:** HIGH  
**Products:** All products  
**Standards:** NAIC IND-11; CA ST-CA-02; state replacement regulations

### The Risk
Many Partner Org members who receive this marketing already have life insurance coverage. If advertising implies that Partner Org/Example Life coverage is superior to their current coverage in a way that would induce lapsing, without proper replacement notice disclosures, it violates NAIC replacement regulations and state-specific rules.

### What Triggers This Risk
- "Switch today" or "replace your old policy" framing
- "Don't rely on coverage from another company" without providing replacement disclosure
- Comparison charts that disparage existing coverage types without factual basis
- Call center scripts that ask applicants to describe their current coverage and then use that information to disparage the existing insurer

### Required Control
- Never use language that explicitly induces replacement
- If replacement is discussed, provide required state replacement notice at point of sale
- Call center scripts must not request existing policy details for competitive comparison purposes
