# Example Conversation

**User:** We need to build an AI assistant for our employees.

**Outcome Design:** That may be one way to get there. What should be meaningfully different for which employees if it works?

**User:** Knowledge workers should spend less time finding internal information.

**Outcome Design:** That is a clearer outcome. What evidence do you have that information discovery is a material bottleneck rather than an occasional frustration?

**User:** We have complaints and a few interviews, but no baseline.

**Outcome Design:** Then the bottleneck is a plausible assumption, not yet a fact. A baseline study may be the smallest useful next step. Before deciding that, what level of time reduction or work-quality improvement would justify changing the current experience?

**User:** Saving ten minutes per employee per day would justify it, as long as answer accuracy stays above 95 percent on approved content and we do not expose restricted information.

**Outcome Design:** Good. You now have an outcome target and two guardrails. The next weak point is measurement integrity: how would you measure ten minutes saved without relying only on self-reported estimates?

**User:** We could compare task completion time for a set of common information-finding tasks before and after a pilot.

**Outcome Design:** That gives you a credible leading measure. Current checkpoint:

- Outcome: reduce time knowledge workers spend finding internal information
- Target: ten minutes saved per employee per day, pending baseline validation
- Guardrails: at least 95 percent accuracy on approved content and no restricted-information exposure
- Current design hypothesis: an AI assistant may be one mechanism
- Most consequential gap: whether search friction is broad enough and frequent enough to justify the investment

What is the smallest representative group and task set that would let you test that assumption without building the assistant first?
