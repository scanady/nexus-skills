# Outcome Pattern Guidance

Use the dominant pattern to sharpen questions without forcing the goal into a rigid template.

## Create

Establish a new capability, state, product, behavior, or experience.

Key questions:

- What can the beneficiary do or experience that is impossible or unreliable today?
- Is the value the new thing itself or the result it enables?
- What adoption or operating conditions must exist after launch?

Common failures: treating delivery as success, ignoring adoption and sustainability, designing an oversized first version.

## Improve

Move an existing condition from a baseline toward a better state.

Key questions:

- What is the verified baseline?
- How much improvement is meaningful?
- What must not degrade while the primary measure improves?

Common failures: no baseline, arbitrary targets, local optimization, regression hidden by averages.

## Reduce

Decrease cost, time, risk, error, harm, or friction.

Key questions:

- Reduce what, for whom, from what baseline, and by when?
- Is the reduction structural or temporary?
- Where might the burden move instead of disappear?

Common failures: cost shifting, quality degradation, one-time savings presented as durable, eliminating useful redundancy.

## Learn

Reduce uncertainty or produce decision-relevant knowledge.

Key questions:

- What decision will the learning enable?
- Which uncertainty is most likely to change that decision?
- What evidence threshold is sufficient?

Common failures: research without a decision, collecting interesting but non-decisive data, proving rather than testing.

## Decide

Choose among alternatives using explicit values, evidence, and trade-offs.

Key questions:

- What outcome should the decision optimize?
- What criteria are mandatory, weighted, or merely desirable?
- Which uncertainty could reverse the choice?
- How reversible is the decision?

Common failures: hidden criteria, false precision, anchoring on a preferred option, ignoring delay or no-action alternatives.

## Change behavior

Create sustained action, habit, adoption, or behavioral change.

Key questions:

- Whose behavior must change, in what context, and for how long?
- What motivation, ability, cues, incentives, and friction shape the behavior?
- What would make the behavior self-sustaining?

Common failures: relying on intention alone, measuring participation rather than sustained behavior, ignoring environment and incentives.

## Maintain

Preserve a valued condition within acceptable boundaries.

Key questions:

- What range is acceptable?
- What early signals indicate deterioration?
- What maintenance burden and resilience are required?

Common failures: no trigger thresholds, hidden decay, maintenance dependent on one person, overreacting to normal variation.

## Recover

Restore an acceptable state after decline, disruption, or failure.

Key questions:

- What state must be restored first?
- What caused the decline and could cause recurrence?
- What recovery time and residual loss are acceptable?

Common failures: restoring symptoms without causes, no stabilization phase, confusing recovery with long-term redesign.

## Combined patterns

Many goals combine patterns. Examples:

- Create a capability to reduce cycle time
- Learn enough to decide whether to proceed
- Recover service and then maintain reliability
- Change behavior to improve health

Identify the primary outcome pattern and treat other patterns as supporting logic.
