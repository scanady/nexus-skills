# Outcome Design Quality Rubric

Use this rubric to steer the review and assess the final record. Ratings guide attention; they are not a substitute for judgment.

## Rating scale

- **Weak:** Missing, vague, contradictory, solution-bound, or unsupported
- **Developing:** Partially defined, but a material gap remains
- **Strong:** Clear and supported enough for the current design stage
- **Not applicable:** Genuinely irrelevant, with a brief reason

Also mark a dimension as a **blocker** when its weakness prevents a responsible downstream handoff.

## Core dimensions

### Outcome clarity

Strong when the future state is specific, meaningful, distinct from an activity or solution, bounded in time and scope, and clear about anti-outcomes.

### Beneficiary and value clarity

Strong when primary beneficiaries, affected parties, intended value, opportunity costs, and goal conflicts are visible.

### Success and measurement integrity

Strong when evidence, baseline, threshold, persistence, guardrails, evidence source, and likely measurement limitations are explicit. Proxies are labeled and tested for gaming or divergence from the actual outcome.

### Reality and evidence quality

Strong when the current state, prior attempts, and material claims are grounded in credible evidence or clearly labeled as interpretations, assumptions, or unknowns.

### Scope and boundary clarity

Strong when inclusions, exclusions, time horizon, controllability, and anti-outcomes prevent accidental expansion or misplaced accountability.

### Ownership and stakeholder realism

Strong when decision rights, contributors, affected parties, incentives, resistance, and accountability are sufficiently understood for the design.

### Assumption and uncertainty discipline

Strong when consequential assumptions are explicit, evidence-calibrated, prioritized by impact and uncertainty, and linked to validation or mitigation.

### Alternative exploration

Strong when alternatives use meaningfully different mechanisms and include credible simpler, delayed, non-technical, and no-action options where relevant.

### Causal coherence

Strong when necessary conditions and the selected mechanism form a plausible theory of change, with competing explanations and attribution limits acknowledged.

### Feasibility and proportionality

Strong when resource envelope, authority, skills, time, dependencies, data, technology, and expected value relative to cost and risk are addressed.

### Progress and learning design

Strong when milestones, leading evidence, review points, and learning steps reveal whether the design is working before the final outcome appears.

### Risk, guardrails, and reversibility

Strong when failure modes, harms, trade-offs, protected conditions, owners, reversibility, and recovery options are visible.

### Rationale quality

Strong when another reviewer can understand key decisions, evidence, challenges, rejected alternatives, accepted trade-offs, and unresolved disagreements.

### Downstream readiness

Strong when the recommended next artifact can be created without reopening foundational questions and its remaining responsibilities are explicit.

## Hard completion checks

Regardless of aggregate quality, do not mark a design downstream-ready when any of these remain unresolved:

- The outcome is still a solution or activity
- Success cannot be distinguished from completion
- A major harm or guardrail is unaddressed
- The selected design has no plausible causal connection to the outcome
- A critical feasibility dependency is unknown and unacknowledged
- A consequential assumption is presented as fact
- The next artifact would need to redefine the goal

## Final assessment format

For each included dimension, provide:

- Rating
- Evidence or gap
- Blocker: yes or no

Then state:

- Maturity
- Disposition
- Most consequential unresolved issue
- Recommended next design action
