# Audience Playbooks

Loaded at Step 2. Board, exec, and peer are built-in. Anything else uses the custom profile process below. The selected playbook (or custom profile) also sets framing depth for the framing block in Step 6.

## Concrete Specifics Are Required, Even With a Built-In

A playbook describes an archetype, not the people in the room. Selecting "board", "exec", or "peer" is never sufficient on its own. Before moving to Step 3, always get at least one or two concrete specifics, for example:

- A known concern this specific audience has raised before (about this topic or an adjacent one)
- Relevant history: has this come up before, and how did it land
- How a specific person in the room is likely to react, if the user knows
- A recent event or decision that's shaping how this audience will hear the story right now

If the user can't supply a specific, ask directly rather than proceeding on the playbook alone: "Is there anything about this particular group, not just execs in general, that would change how this lands?" A thin answer is fine to proceed on. No answer at all is not; push once more before moving to Step 3.

## Board

**What they weigh:** fiduciary duty, risk, governance, precedent, how this compares to what other companies in the position have done.

**Likely objections:** "What's the downside case", "who else has done this", "what does this commit us to".

**Framing depth:** tightest of the three. Driver, ask(s), and takeaway(s) in a handful of sentences each. Assume no appetite for process detail unless it bears on risk or governance.

## Exec

**What they weigh:** strategic alignment, resourcing and cost, what this trades off against other priorities, who owns execution.

**Likely objections:** "Why this over the other things on my list", "what does this cost us", "who's accountable if it slips".

**Framing depth:** moderate. Driver and ask(s) stated directly, takeaway(s) can carry a bit more supporting context than the board version, especially around resourcing.

## Peer

**What they weigh:** technical credibility, whether the approach holds up under scrutiny, how this affects their own work.

**Likely objections:** "Have you considered X", "how does this interact with what my team owns", "what's the evidence this works".

**Framing depth:** lightest constraint of the three. Framing block can be shorter relative to the body, since peers are often reading for the reasoning and evidence more than the headline.

## Custom Profile

When the audience isn't board, exec, or peer, elicit:
- Who they are (role, relationship to the presenter)
- What they currently believe about the topic, going in
- What they weigh most when deciding or evaluating something like this
- Their likely objection or point of skepticism
- A rough framing depth call: tight (headline-first, minimal buildup), moderate, or detailed (comfortable with more context up front)

Record the custom profile inline in the story brief's Audience section rather than trying to force it into one of the three built-ins.
