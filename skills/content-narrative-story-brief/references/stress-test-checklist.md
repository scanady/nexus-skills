# Stress Test Checklist

Run at Step 5, before assembling the final brief.

## So-What Test (per beat)

For every beat in the arc-developed body, ask: if this beat were removed, would the story still reach its outcome(s)? If yes, the beat is decoration, not argument. Either cut it, fold it into the supporting-detail appendix, or sharpen it until it's doing real work.

A beat passes when it changes what the audience believes or does next, not just when it's true and related to the topic.

## One-Sentence Logline Test (whole story)

State the entire story in one sentence: driver, ask or takeaway, and why it should land. If it can't compress, the story likely has more than one story trying to happen at once, or an outcome that's still too vague to argue for.

Example shape: "Because [driver], we're asking [audience] to [ask], which matters because [why]."

If there are multiple outcomes (up to 3, per Step 1), the logline should still name the throughline connecting them, not just list them side by side. If no throughline exists, the outcomes may not belong in the same story.

## Failing Either Test

Don't patch a failing beat or logline with more words. Go back to Step 1 or Step 4 and revise the underlying outcome or beat rather than smoothing over the symptom in the write-up.
