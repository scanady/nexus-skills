# CS Team Evolution

Decision: which CS role do we hire next, and how does CS differ from support, account management, and implementation?

Wrong question: "CSM or support engineer?" Right question: "Which customer outcome are we failing to deliver, and which role unblocks it?" Hire against a named failure, not a job title.

## Six roles

| Role | Owns | Does not own |
|---|---|---|
| Customer support | Reactive issue resolution, ticket queue, first response, product knowledge | Renewal, expansion, proactive outreach |
| CSM | Proactive value, renewal, expansion lead | Daily tickets, technical implementation |
| Account manager | Commercial relationship, expansion close, contract talks | Daily success work, technical depth, tickets |
| Implementation manager | Onboarding, go-live, first value | Success after launch (hands to CSM) |
| CS operations | Tools, data, analytics, playbooks, health scores | Direct customer relationships |
| Customer marketing | Advocacy, case studies, references, events | One-to-one relationships, renewal |

Common mix-ups: CSM is not support (proactive vs reactive). CSM plus AM in one seat is risky (success lens vs commercial lens). CSM is not implementation (launch is bounded; success is ongoing).

## Five stages

| Stage | Size | CS team | Next hire | Do not hire yet | Move on when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Pre-PMF / seed | 1-15 people | none; founders do CS | None. Founder customer contact is a feature | CSM, support, AM | Founder spends over 40% of week on customer issues, 10+ paying customers, post-sale playbook is clear |
| 2. Series A | 15-50 | 1-3 | 1st CSM (not support). 2nd: support specialist once 30+ customers. 3rd: implementation (often shared) at higher ACV | AM, CS ops, customer marketing | 100+ customers, $1M+ ARR, 3+ CSMs, real segmentation |
| 3. Series B | 50-200 | 4-10 | CS manager (promote internally); CS ops (CSMs lose 30%+ time to tooling and reporting); AM if CSMs lose renewals and ACV justifies; customer marketing at 5+ public references | | 250+ customers, $5M+ ARR, 8+ CS staff, several tiers |
| 4. Growth | 200-1,000 | 10-50 | Director or VP of CS (10+ staff, NRR in board story); tier-specialised CSMs; separate implementation team; renewals team if CSMs drown in contract work | | CS is in company strategy; one leader needed for CX |
| 5. Late stage | 1,000+ | 50-300+ | CCO; customer operations as one function (CS ops, support ops, customer marketing ops, customer data); federated CSMs embedded in product, vertical, region | | |

Profiles: first CSM has 3-5 years of B2B SaaS CS, product fluency, comfort with no playbook. Support hire: technical, empathic, writes docs. CS ops: SQL and spreadsheets, light scripting, has run CRM workflows. AM: closer with commercial skill. CCO: has run CS at $100M+ ARR and reports on retention to the board.

## AM versus CSM split

Split when: ACV $20K+; CSMs avoid commercial work and lose renewals; cross-sell motion is distinct; GTM is sales-led.
Do not split when: mid-market or lower; PLG; small team; founder still near deals.
Hybrid (most common): CSM owns relationship and renewal; AM only closes complex expansion; commission shared between CSM who found it and AM who closed it.

## Anti-patterns

- Support as first CS hire (solves a problem you may not have yet; CSM solves a day-one problem)
- CS ops before CSMs (nothing to operate; need emerges from CSM friction)
- Top CSM promoted to manager with no training
- CSM plus AM merged forever (works under $5M ARR; breaks above; plan the split)
- CSM as "support plus": tickets routed to CSMs because they know the customer; destroys proactive time. Route tickets to support
- Customer marketing under CS (usually reports to marketing)
- CCO under $10M ARR (political role with nothing to run)

## Sequencing rule

Hire the next CS role only when: (1) current role is filled and ramped (3-6 months in seat); (2) that role shipped a named customer outcome; (3) you can name the gap the next hire fills.

## Roadmap exercise (1 week)

1. List top five customer outcomes the company fails to deliver
2. Map each to the role that unblocks it
3. Sequence hires by prerequisite order
4. Check cost and level with finance and people leads

Out of scope: pay benchmarks, career ladders, CS tool selection, performance management.
