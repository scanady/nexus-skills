# E-E-A-T Checklist

E-E-A-T = Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. It comes from Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines. It is a lens for judging content, not a single ranking factor and not a score Google publishes. Audit the observable signals below and say so in the report; never write "your E-E-A-T score is X".

**Trust is the anchor.** The other three feed it. A knowledgeable page that hides who runs the site or makes false claims fails on trust.

## Signals to check

| Signal | Evidence to find | Pass looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Experience | First-hand detail: own photos, screenshots, test results, "what went wrong" | Specifics a researcher could not get from other pages |
| Expertise | Author bio, credentials relevant to the topic, accurate and current facts | Named author, bio linked from the byline, depth fits the topic |
| Authoritativeness | Citations by others, links from respected sites, press, speaker or publication record | The brand or author is referenced as a source in its field |
| Trust | HTTPS, contact details, About page, policies, sources cited, honest claims, visible updated date | A reader can tell who is accountable and can check the facts |

Authoritativeness lives off-site. Without a backlink or mention tool, mark it "not assessed".

## Page-level checklist

**Authorship**
- [ ] Byline names a real person (or a clear organization for corporate content)
- [ ] Bio page exists, states relevant background, links to other work
- [ ] Reviewer named for medical, legal, or financial content

**Substance**
- [ ] Original observations, data, or examples, not a rewrite of the top results
- [ ] Claims carry linked sources; sources are primary where possible
- [ ] No misleading headline, hidden sponsorship, or undisclosed affiliate links

**Site-level trust**
- [ ] HTTPS, no browser warnings
- [ ] Contact route (email, form, address) one click from every page
- [ ] About page says who owns the site and why it exists
- [ ] Privacy policy and terms present; returns and shipping policy for shops
- [ ] `dateModified` or a visible "updated" date, kept true (not auto-bumped)
- [ ] Reviews and testimonials are real and attributable

## YMYL pages

YMYL = "Your Money or Your Life": health, finance, legal, safety, major purchases, and news that affects civic decisions. Raters hold these to the highest bar.

- Author credentials must be verifiable, not self-claimed.
- Content must agree with expert consensus, with sources.
- Missing credentials on a YMYL page is `critical`; on other pages the same gap is `high` or `medium`.

## Scorer mapping (category `content`)

| Check | Severity |
|---|---|
| Author bio present and linked | high |
| Sources cited with links | medium |
| Updated date within 12 months, for topics that change | medium |
| Qualified, verifiable author on a YMYL page | critical |
| Contact information reachable | medium |

The 12-month window is a working rule. Evergreen pages can pass without a recent date; fast-moving topics should not.

## Common fixes

- Build real author pages and link every byline to them.
- Add a "How we tested" or "Sources" block.
- Replace stock photos with original images where first-hand experience is the claim.
- Add a review and update note to sensitive pages.
- Remove or fix pages that cannot be made trustworthy.
