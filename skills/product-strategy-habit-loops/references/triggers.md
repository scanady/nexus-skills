# Triggers

Triggers are the actuators of behavior — no trigger, no action. Two kinds: **external** (in the environment) and **internal** (from within the user). The strategic arc is external → internal.

## External Triggers

Embedded with information telling the user what to do next.

| Type | What | Best for |
|------|------|----------|
| Paid | ads, SEM | acquisition |
| Earned | PR, viral, app-store features | growth spikes |
| Relationship | word of mouth, referrals | trust-based acquisition |
| Owned | your channels — push, email, badges | retention |

Design principles: be specific about the next action ("See 3 new matches", not "Check out our app"); create relevance, not generic urgency ("Your weekly report is ready"); tie to the internal trigger ("5 friends posted while you were away", not "We miss you!"); respect attention (meaningful events, not everything).

Owned channels trade off reach vs intrusion — push (immediate but easily disabled), email (rich but low open rates), SMS (high open but invasive, critical only), in-app badges (subtle), browser notifications (ask after value shown). Audit each send: does it name the action, connect to the internal trigger, lead to a reward, arrive at the right time, at a sustainable frequency — and would *you* want to receive it?

## Internal Triggers

Cues from within — emotions, routines, situations. The goal: users prompt themselves.

```
External trigger → repeated use → association → internal trigger
```

| Emotion/state | Wants | Product example |
|---------------|-------|-----------------|
| Boredom | stimulation | TikTok, Instagram |
| Loneliness | connection | WhatsApp, Discord |
| Uncertainty | information | Google, Reddit |
| FOMO | not to miss out | Twitter, Slack |
| Stress | relief | games, meditation |
| Seeking validation | approval | social media |
| Incompleteness | closure/progress | to-do apps, Duolingo |

**Find it with 5-Whys** — keep asking "why" until you land on an emotion (e.g. "find recipes" → … → "they feel stuck and uninspired"). Build the association through consistent pairing (emotion → product), immediate relief (delays weaken it), frequency (daily > weekly), and stable context (same situation + emotion). Discover it by asking users: what were you doing right before, what prompted you, how did you feel, what would you have done otherwise, when in the day, what situation.

## The Transition

Three stages, each with an action:
1. **External dependency** — relies on prompts → send consistent, valuable external triggers. (High notification CTR, low organic opens.)
2. **Forming association** — begins linking product to emotion → reinforce the emotional connection in-product. (Growing organic sessions.)
3. **Internal established** — thinks of the product automatically → reduce external triggers, deepen the habit. (Consistent unprompted usage.)

## Common Mistakes
- Too many notifications → quality over quantity (users disable/uninstall)
- Generic triggers → personalize to the user's state
- Wrong timing → send when relevant to the emotion
- Unclear action → specific, actionable message
- No internal-trigger strategy → forever renting attention
- Ignoring opt-outs → respect preferences immediately
